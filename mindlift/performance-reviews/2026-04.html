We've seen this exact pattern before — boosts typically decay; the brand with longer compounding history (yours) usually retakes ground organically.

This is a healthy mix for a 10-month-old business — Google is sending you the customers searching for the category, not just searching for your name. The opportunity ahead: brand-awareness investment (PR, social, OOH, the city-mention review tactic) would compound the existing SEO lead and lift the branded share over time.

3. How Local SEO Translates to Revenue

💻

Website Clicks → Online Orders → Pickup & Delivery

Across Dec 2025 – May 2026, MindLift's GBP delivered 6,306 website clicks — the dominant action type (74.8% of all profile interactions). With your menu hosted on Jane (mindliftdispensary.com/menu) and the GBP link UTM-tagged for tracking, this is the primary digital-to-purchase pipeline.

At MindLift's April Treez AOV of ~$58, even a modest 5–10% conversion of monthly website clicks (~1,200 in April) maps to roughly 60–120 attributable orders and $3.5K–$7K of revenue per month — and that's a conservative read. The website click channel is doing real work.

📍

Direction Requests → Walk-In + Pickup

1,457 direction requests over the 6-month window (17.3% of all GBP actions). April hit ~300 in a single month — its highest. Directions are the strongest leading indicator of an in-person visit, whether that's a walk-in for an in-store shopping session or a stop to pick up an online order.

This channel is structurally smaller than website clicks for MindLift (consistent with delivery-first operations), but it's still a meaningful share of the customer mix and growing.

📞

Phone Calls → High-Intent Conversations

568 calls from GBP across 6 months. April delivered ~120 — the highest monthly call volume. Phone calls represent the highest-effort pre-visit action: customers picking up the phone with a specific question. Historically these convert to in-store visits or delivery orders at higher rates than passive channels.

April's call volume was 1.81× the Q4 2025 monthly average. Combined with the 388 new customers in April, this signals a meaningful inflow of new-customer interest reaching out before their first visit.

⭐

Reviews + 5.0★ Rating → Cross-Channel Conversion Engine

You closed April with 624 Google reviews at 5.0★ — by far the best-rated dispensary in your competitive set (Kota Canna is at 76 reviews, Plant Base at 176, Queen City at 486). Your review velocity has averaged ~62 reviews per month since opening — right on the 60/month cadence target.

Customer reviews repeatedly mention the team's friendliness, the speed of service, and — notably — your "no delivery fee, no order minimum" policy. The 5.0★ at 624 reviews is what makes every other digital channel work harder: visitors arriving from any source see best-in-market social proof before they decide to engage.

6. May 2026 Workstreams from Dispenza

April's story is clear: the business is firing on all cylinders. May's work focuses on (a) defending the dispensary-term position against Kota Canna's new-business boost, (b) building the website infrastructure to fully amplify the brand differentiators that are currently invisible to organic visitors, and (c) anchoring the upcoming Phillipsburg satellite GBP with the right website foundation. Six workstreams:

Technical SEO Foundation — Tier 1 Fixes

The May 12 site audit surfaced foundational gaps that limit how much Google can amplify the brand. We're shipping: missing robots.txt, sitewide JSON-LD schema markup (LocalBusiness, Organization, FAQPage on the 12 visible FAQs), an H1 on the homepage (currently missing), fixed double-H1 on /about-us, and a tightened title tag for the About page (currently 150 chars). These are the technical blockers that let Google understand and feature the site properly.

Brand Differentiator Content — Surface What Makes MindLift Unique

Right now the website doesn't mention "Black-owned", "Woman-owned", "Asian-owned", "LGBTQ+ friendly", "24/7 delivery", or "no minimum / no delivery fee" anywhere on the home, about, delivery, or location pages. These are your strongest competitive moats — and they're invisible to organic visitors. We're rewriting /about-us (600+ words), /delivery (1,000+ words leading with 24/7), and the Plainfield store landing page to make these the headline message.

Phillipsburg Landing Page — Anchor the Satellite GBP

Your Phillipsburg satellite GBP launch needs a corresponding website landing page to anchor entity relevance with Google. Currently the /delivery page mentions Phillipsburg and has an "ORDER PHILLIPSBURG" CTA, but there's no dedicated landing page. We're building /phillipsburg in May so the satellite has the SEO anchor it needs when it goes live.

Cannabis + Dispensary Term Defense — Counter Kota Canna

While the new-business boost helping Kota Canna will typically decay in 6–12 months, we're not waiting passively. May content focuses on cannabis-leading language in the homepage hero, GBP description rewrite featuring "cannabis dispensary" terminology, GBP posts using "dispensary near me" / "cannabis store" language in image alt text, and 4 dedicated blog posts targeting cannabis/dispensary intent. Defense + content acceleration in parallel.

GBP Cadence Sustain + Photo Refresh

GBP posts at 4+/week, weekly photo uploads (storefront, delivery, interactions, product drops), and a Direct-vs-Discovery KPI split in weekly reporting. We'll also surface the photo refresh need (see retailer recommendations below — the storefront photo currently shows "LIFT DISPENSARY" signage and a construction dumpster).

Dispenza ROI Tool ACV Calibration

Your Dispenza admin ROI tool currently uses a $50 Average Cart Value assumption. Your actual Treez AOV is ~$57.94 in April (~$53 net basis). We're updating the configuration in May so future ROI reports reflect the true ~$55 baseline — which lifts your reported attributed revenue ~10% (TGS rises from $19.7K to ~$21.7K, ROI rises from 197% to ~217%). 7. Recommendations for the MindLift Team

Our audit surfaced opportunities that are best executed by your in-store team rather than by Dispenza — they depend on photo refresh decisions, in-store training, POS configuration, or regulatory checks that your team is best positioned to make. We surface them here for your consideration. These are observations, not Dispenza commitments.

📸 Storefront Photo Refresh — Visible Brand Mismatch

What we found: The current GBP storefront photo shows signage that reads "LIFT DISPENSARY", not "MindLift Recreational Weed Dispensary". The photo also shows a yellow construction dumpster outside the entrance — likely an old image from before the storefront was complete.

What you might consider: A fresh storefront photo capture (well-lit afternoon, clean exterior, current signage) plus a few interior shots — counter, display cases, lounge area. The current photo is the first visual a discovery customer sees on Google Maps; ensuring it matches the brand reduces walk-up confusion.

Dispenza supports this by: we'll upload and order the photo gallery in GBP, write the alt text, and tie it into the May post calendar.

🚗 Curbside Pickup — Worth a Regulatory Check

What we found: Your GBP service options include in-store shopping, in-store pickup for online orders, and delivery — but not curbside pickup. This is the only service-option gap.

What you might consider: A quick check with regulatory counsel on whether NJ adult-use rules permit cannabis curbside pickup. If permitted, it's a low-cost addition that materially improves convenience for mobility-limited customers, parents-with-kids-in-car, and weather-day pickup. If not permitted in NJ, the gap is regulatory, not operational — and we leave it as documented.

💳 Credit Card Acceptance — Confirm Before Amplifying in Marketing

What we found: Your GBP "More" tab lists VISA, Discover, AmEx, Mastercard, Diners Club, plus NFC mobile payments. This is rare for cannabis retail (federally illegal status means most card networks don't process direct cannabis charges) and would be a meaningful customer-experience differentiator if accurate.

What you might consider: A confirmation from your operations team on the current payment-processor setup. If it's via a cashless-ATM workaround or a compliant processor like Aeropay, the messaging needs to reflect that nuance. Once confirmed, this becomes prime marketing material (and material for your About page rewrite).

📝 Review-Velocity City Mentions — Stretch the Ranking Radius

The data: You're at 624 reviews at 5.0★ with a ~62/month organic velocity. Excellent already. The opportunity is in the content of new reviews, not the volume.

What you might consider: A simple budtender / driver script for delivery customers — "If you have a minute to leave us a review, please mention which town we delivered to today. It helps neighbors in your area find us." Google uses city-mentions in reviews as a proximity-relevance signal that can extend your algorithmic ranking radius beyond Plainfield. Specifically valuable for: Phillipsburg (when the satellite GBP launches), Scotch Plains, Edison, South Plainfield, and the broader Union County / North Jersey service area.

Dispenza supports this by: we'll supply the suggested budtender/driver script and design a small register placard reinforcing the ask.
