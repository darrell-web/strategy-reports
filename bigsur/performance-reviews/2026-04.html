2. Diagnosis: Is the Decline a Market Issue or a Ranking Issue?

The right answer matters because it determines whether the fix is "wait it out" or "execute against a clear plan." The data points to the second.

2.1 The April Top-Search Terms Tell the Story

Below are Big Sur's top 7 search queries in April 2026, overlaid against where Big Sur ranks for each on Google's 8-mile local grid (from the May 1 rank scan):

The diagnosis: The two highest-volume search terms in April — "dispensary near me" (408) and "dispensary" (407), combined 815 searches — are exactly the terms where Big Sur currently ranks ~#5 in the local pack. Meanwhile, the terms Big Sur dominates at #1 (weed / weed near me / weed dispensary) have a combined April volume of ~325. The lost impressions are not the market drying up — they are search demand existing at high volume but being captured by competitors who outrank Big Sur on those specific queries.

2.2 The Branded-vs-Discovery Pattern Confirms the Diagnosis

If this were a market collapse, both branded and non-branded searches would fall together. They didn't:

  • Branded (Direct) searches: +11.1% YoY — people who already know Big Sur are still searching for the name, more this April than last April. Brand awareness is compounding.
  • Discovery (non-branded) searches: -39.7% YoY — the entire impression decline is concentrated in non-branded discovery, where ranking on generic terms is the dominant factor.

This is the classic signature of a ranking-driven impression loss, not a market collapse. When customers know who you are, they find you. When customers search generically for the category, ranking is everything.

2.3 The Market Component (Real, But Smaller)

We acknowledge two real market dynamics also at play, sized smaller than the ranking gap:

  • Element 7 Monterey's market entry — accumulating 223 reviews in 2 months has fragmented some local discovery demand. This is a competitive market dynamic, but it manifests as ranking pressure on Big Sur, not as a vanishing market.
  • California Maps query normalization — broader Maps search volume has softened modestly across the state. We estimate this contributes ~10 percentage points of the YoY decline.

The remaining ~15 percentage points is what we believe ranking work will recover. The market is still searching; the question is who they find first.

The fix is already in flight. The May 1 Local Falcon rank scan showed positive movement on every one of the gap terms — "dispensary" SoLV improved +2.86 points, "cannabis near me" SoLV improved +1.43 points, "dispensary near me" average rank improved 0.19 positions. We are not starting from zero; we are accelerating a trend already moving the right direction.

3. The May Plan to Recover Impressions

Dispenza is executing a multi-front plan in May to push the gap terms toward parity with the weed-side wins. The specific actions:

1

Push "Cannabis Near Me" Past 70% SoLV

This term is the lowest-hanging fruit — currently 64.3% SoLV with positive momentum (+1.43 pts vs prior scan). May actions: (1) confirm primary GBP category is "Cannabis store" — already correct, (2) add three secondary GBP categories (Marijuana dispensary, CBD store, Alternative medicine practitioner), (3) revise GBP description to lead with cannabis terminology in the first 100 characters.

2

Schema Deepening + FAQ Markup

Add Product schema for top-20 SKUs (WYLD-led) and Offer schema for active price points. Add FAQ schema on Carmel, Pacific Grove, Monterey, Pebble Beach, and Cannery Row landing pages with question structure like "What's the closest dispensary to [landmark]?" — directly capturing the high-volume "dispensary near me" intent geographically.

3

Three New Tourism-Intent Blog Pieces

"Cannabis Near Highway 1" (week 2), "Memorial Day at Pebble Beach" (week 3), "Cannabis for Pebble Beach Golf Days" (week 4). These pieces give Google fresh on-domain content keyed to the highest-volume cannabis/dispensary terms paired with the landmark anchors tourists actually search.

4

Sustained Review Velocity

Refreshed QR placards at register; budtender review-ask script live in week 1. Target 15+ new reviews in May. We are not engaging in a reviews arms race with Element 7 — 15/month is sustainable and matches what the algorithm rewards over the long arc.

4. How April's GBP Activity Connected to Customer Flow

📍

Directions Requests Held Strong → Walk-In Volume Stable

April directions requests came in at 424 — essentially flat vs April 2025 (446, -4.9%) despite the broader 21.7% impression decline. This is meaningful: the customers who are finding Big Sur are still committing to the trip. Walk-in remains 87.8% of all lifetime transactions ($7.07M of $8.34M).

April POS recorded 4,928 walk-in transactions. The gap to close in May is the ratio between "directions requested" and "transaction completed" — basket-building and budtender upselling are the levers there.

📞

Phone Calls Up Month-over-Month

April phone calls hit 78, up from 77 in March — a continued recovery from the February low of 70. Phone calls are the highest-intent customer signal short of a walk-in. Trajectory is positive heading into Memorial Day weekend.

🔁

Branded Search Growth → Long-Term Brand Compounding

Branded searches grew +11.1% YoY while non-branded declined. This is the clearest proof that the long-term SEO investment is building genuine brand awareness — customers are remembering the name and returning to it deliberately. Brand-driven traffic converts at materially higher rates and lower acquisition cost than discovery traffic.

💰

Loyalty Engagement → Repeat Customer Strength

Lifetime loyalty data shows 8.33M points earned and 5.72M redeemed — a 68.7% redemption rate that is exceptionally high in cannabis retail. Combined with 27,156 lifetime new members, the loyalty engine is the right place to invest in May for margin-positive customer reactivation.

Close the Cannabis/Dispensary Rank Gap (Priority 1)

Capture Peak Tourism Season
