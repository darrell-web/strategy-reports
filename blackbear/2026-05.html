The patterns that matter

PA discovery softening is reversible

The GBP name change removed "weed" from titles. Regent Square alone lost ~2,500 monthly "weed dispensary" searches. Compounded by months of bare shelves from the PA vendor consolidation. Shelves are now at 75–80% of normal depth and rebuilding. The listings need to signal that recovery; rankings will trail by 4–6 weeks.

Winona is the highest-conversion asset in the portfolio

65.8% conversion in April is the highest opening month we've recorded across any client. 88% of those actions go to the website. Translation: the .com is where Winona makes or loses every dollar. Fixing the state picker + caching bugs is the single highest-ROI move in May.

Meadville's vape window is open right now

"Vape shop near me" sits in the top 10 of Meadville's search queries — unprecedented across the entire Dispenza portfolio. Cause: the surrounding vape shops were raided (hemp products seized). Black Bear is the last vape retailer of consequence in town. This window will close as competitors backfill. Treat it as a 2026-Q2/Q3 land-grab.

Brand recall is the cushion

56–65% of search volume is branded (direct) — exceptional. Customers know the name. The job for May isn't building brand awareness — it's converting more of that searched-by-name traffic and recapturing the discovery layer lost to the title change.

The strategic bet for May

May's thesis
Two markets, two rule sets, one playbook.
In PA, defend the brand through the algorithm shift while shelf depth recovers — content + photos do the work. In MN, remove every friction point between a 65% conversion rate and Winona's first $1M month — fix the site, ship the page, capture every "weed" search Google will allow.

PA — Defend & Diversify

Photo refresh (every store, every week) · 4 content pieces · 2 location-page rewrites · review velocity push at Bridgeville · vape category amplification at Meadville · CuraLeaf interception at Clarion.

MN — Remove Friction

Fix 5 website bugs · ship /winona/ and /shop/store-menus/winona/ · keep "Weed Dispensary" in GBP + change Winona site subheader to match · UTM the GBP website link · Flowhub integration once credentials land.

Why one playbook isn't enough

The portfolio has split into two markets that behave nothing alike:

  • PA is regulated, branded, recovering, and processor-sensitive. The naming convention is "Hemp Dispensary" because PA payment processors enforce against "weed." The state vendor list controls what we can stock and sell. Our job is to retain market share while shelves rebuild.
  • MN is unregulated-light, unbranded, growing, and search-driven. Jesse's directive on May 13: keep "Weed Dispensary" in the GBP and force Google to remove it if they will. The Winona subheader changes the other direction — from "Hemp" to "Weed" — because MN customers search for "weed."
Reading the data as one portfolio will lead to bad decisions. Reading them as two markets sharing a brand will lead to right ones.

The math behind the headline KPIs

KPI 1 · Winona 4,500 BPI

Apr 13 → Apr 30: 2,698 BPI in 18 days = ~150 BPI/day. Maintaining the post-opening daily baseline of 100–150 across 31 May days yields a floor of 3,100 (low) → 4,650 (high). Stretch to 5,000 requires ~161/day average. Mother's Day, Memorial Day, and graduation season are tailwinds; the 5 website bugs are headwinds that should be removed in week 1.

Sensitivity: Every full day the state-picker bug persists is ~88% × ~150 = ~132 lost website sessions at current run rate. Five lost days = 660 sessions = roughly one full week of organic discovery wasted.

KPI 2 · Meadville +30% vs April

Apr BPI estimate: ~660 (April share of Jan–Apr 2,505). Target May BPI: ~860. Vape category contribution: "vape shop near me" already in top 10 (~80 monthly impressions) → with a Meadville vape blog + 4 GBP graphics targeting vape + 710 (July 10 dab day) early-build, we expect "vape" terms to compound 30–50% across May–June.

Why this is High confidence: Meadville already grew +27% YoY against the portfolio's worst macro headwind. The vape opportunity is incremental, not foundational.

KPI 3 · Regent Square + Bridgeville back to ≥35% conversion

Current conversion in the 16–18% range. Portfolio baseline is 35%. Conversion is a listing-hygiene problem, not a discovery problem — both stores get plenty of profile views (Regent ~21K, Bridgeville ~21K). The fix: weekly photo cadence + GBP post cadence + Bridgeville review velocity + Bridgeville chat re-attempt on a new number.

Conversion lift mechanics: a 5pp lift on Regent Square alone (16.8% → 21.8%) = +1,070 monthly bottom-funnel actions at current views.

KPI 4 · Bridgeville +10 reviews in May, recency < 7 days

Bridgeville sits at 35 reviews, latest ~6 weeks old. Review recency is one of the three known Google Local algorithm signals (alongside proximity and prominence). 10 new reviews at ~3.2/mo baseline velocity is a 3× lift — achievable with QR codes + budtender prompts.

Caveat (live finding from May 13): Google may be starting to penalize budtender-name-tagged reviews as solicitation signal. Darrell investigating. Strategy will adjust if confirmed.
